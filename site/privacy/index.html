Privacy

Daylo Privacy Policy

Effective September 7, 2026 · Applies to the Daylo app (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android), the web demo, and this website.

The short version: Daylo does not collect your data. Everything you enter stays on your device. The app has no account system, no server of its own, and no analytics. This website uses privacy-respecting, cookieless analytics that never identify you.

What the app stores, and where

Daylo stores the activities you create, the days you mark as done, and your settings. This data lives only in the app's local storage on your device. It is not uploaded, synced, or backed up by us. If you uninstall the app, the data is deleted with it. Use Export Data inside the app to keep a copy first.

The web demo works the same way, except the data lives in your browser's local storage on this website's domain. Clearing your browser data removes it.

What the app sends

Nothing. Daylo makes no network requests of its own. There is no telemetry, no crash reporting, and no third-party SDK inside the app.

If we add an optional usage signal in the future

We may add a "Share anonymous usage" switch in the app's settings. It will be off by default and asked about once. If you turn it on, the app would send, at most once a week, to our own self-hosted analytics server:

  • the app version and platform (for example "1.2.0, Android");
  • a random identifier generated on your device so we can count how many devices keep using Daylo over time. It is not derived from anything about you or your device, is never linked to your name, email, or activities, and is deleted if you turn the switch off.

Nothing you track, such as activity names, dates or notes, is ever included. If this feature ships, this page will be updated to say so on the day it does.

This is not just a promise. Daylo's public code is checked automatically on every change: the check fails if any analytics, tracking or error-reporting service or library appears in the app's code, its dependencies or its built files. You can see that check in the project's GitHub repository.

This website

daylo.henfrydls.com uses Umami, an open-source analytics tool that we host ourselves at analytics.henfrydls.com. It records page views and which download button was clicked, in aggregate. Umami does not use cookies, does not store your IP address, does not fingerprint your browser, and does not track you across other sites. There is no advertising and no data is shared with third parties.

Downloads

Release files are hosted on GitHub. When you download from the GitHub Releases page, GitHub's privacy statement applies to that request. If Daylo is later available through an app store (Google Play, Microsoft Store, F-Droid, Flathub), that store's own policy applies to the download and update process; Daylo itself still sends nothing.

Children

Daylo collects no personal data from anyone, including children. It has no age gate because it has no accounts and nothing to protect.

Your rights

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export, or delete on your behalf. Your data is on your device, under your control: export it, back it up, or delete the app.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new effective date. Substantive changes, such as adding the optional usage signal, will also be announced in the release notes on GitHub.

Contact

Open an issue at github.com/henfrydls/daylo/issues, or write to the contact address published on henfrydls.com.
